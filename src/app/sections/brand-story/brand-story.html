Most furniture is designed to be looked at. This is designed to be lived around, which means the drawing starts with what is left over: the clearance beneath a seat, the wall a chair backs onto, the width of a walkway kept open. A piece is finished when the space around it still works, and that is a harder thing to draw than the piece itself.

Everyday life is the only brief worth taking seriously. A sofa has to survive a Tuesday as well as a photograph. A surface has to take a cup ring, a scratch and a decade of daylight without turning into either a museum exhibit or a disposal problem. Anything that cannot do that has not been designed so much as styled.

The name is the argument. A nova is the point at which a quantity of light suddenly becomes enough to see by — something that was always there, and only now is legible. A good room does the same thing. Nothing moves. A little of the right thing arrives in the right place, and the whole space reads more clearly than it did before.

  • Nothing added for effect.
  • One collection, drawn as a set.
